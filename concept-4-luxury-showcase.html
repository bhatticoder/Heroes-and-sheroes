The Main Feature

Serving the Most Vulnerable

The Tim Tebow Foundation — faith, hope and love, in three disciplined parts.
2010Founded
Three-PartPrevention · Rescue · Survivor Care
GlobalU.S. & international partners

Founded in 2010, the Tim Tebow Foundation describes its mission as bringing faith, hope and love to people facing some of the darkest times in their lives — with a focus on the “Most Vulnerable People” around the world.

One of its major areas of work is the fight against human trafficking and child exploitation. The approach has three parts: prevention through education and training; rescue through partnerships supporting rescue missions and immediate survivor needs; and survivor care focused on longer-term recovery and support — including communities of care and safe homes where people rebuild their lives with dignity.

Heroes & Sheros Magazine features the foundation as a heroic organization because its work matches our mission: honoring the people and organizations that serve vulnerable communities — quietly, consistently, and over time.

Every claim in this feature is checked against the foundation’s official information before publication. It is our standard for every story, in every issue, in every edition.

“Bringing faith, hope and love to people facing some of the darkest times in their lives.” — Tim Tebow Foundation

T.I. & Tiny Harris — 143 Homes in Atlanta

T.I., also known as Clifford Joseph Harris Jr., is an Atlanta rapper, actor and entrepreneur. Tameka “Tiny” Harris is a singer and songwriter who rose to prominence as a member of Xscape and is also known for her songwriting.

Atlanta Housing reported that Intrada Westside opened in November 2023 as a 100-percent affordable housing community with 143 residential units, including 25 designated for permanent supportive housing. T.I. participated in the ribbon-cutting ceremony alongside Atlanta Mayor Andre Dickens and other project partners.

Lifestyle & Editorial

Where Honor Meets Lifestyle

There is a way of giving that changes the giver as much as the receiver. It looks like a long table at a community dinner. It looks like a sponsorship fund with a family’s name on it. It looks like a child in a wheelchair, photographed with the same dignity as any cover star — because in this magazine, dignity is the house style.

This section is where philanthropy catches its breath: the causes our honorees carry, the partnerships that make them possible, and the quiet texture of a life lived generously. Intrada Westside’s 143 homes are an editorial about the future. The Tim Tebow Foundation’s safe houses are an editorial about protection. This page is the editorial about the space between them — thanks, follow-through, and standards.

“Giving well is a craft. We treat it like one.”
