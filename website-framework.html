Honoree index

Nominate someone

How a honoree page reads

  • Name, one-line role, and the city they serve.
  • A verified biography in three paragraphs, with dates checked against public record.
  • The nominator's story, published only with permission and a contact trail.
  • Portrait with credit and usage permission noted in the caption.

Editorial rules on the page

  • No honoree is published before the editorial office confirms the facts.
  • Corrections run on the page, dated, with the old wording archived.
  • Current political figures appear as factual reporting with sourcing, without endorsement.
  • Any quote awaiting sign-off stays marked Sample in mockups.

Nominate a hero or shero

See the nominations email

The form asks four things

  • The nominee's name, role and city.
  • What they did, in your own words, with a date if you remember one.
  • How you know them, and how the editorial office can reach you.
  • Whether we may publish your name as the nominator.

What happens next

  • The editorial office reviews every nomination before anything is published.
  • We contact the nominee before a word runs. form and confirmation flow to confirm
  • Published honorees appear in the issue index and stay in the archive.

Serving the most vulnerable

Heroes & Sheros staff | Sourced from the foundation's public record | Sample copy

Founded in 2010, the Tim Tebow Foundation describes its mission as bringing faith, hope and love to people facing some of the darkest times in their lives.

Every article on the site follows one shape: a headline that names the subject, a byline with the reporting basis, a standfirst that states the verified fact, then the story in full. Images carry captions with credit and permission status, and the piece ends with its sources in a box readers can check.

The template keeps politics on the factual side of the line. Stories about current political figures run as reporting with clear sourcing and no endorsement, and corrections appear dated on the same page.
