Editor's Note | 03
A letter from the Chief Editor

Why we give honor

Heroes & Sheros Magazine is built around one simple idea: people who make a difference deserve to be seen, heard, and honored. In every issue we shine a light on individuals, organizations, families, professionals and everyday people whose actions show compassion, courage, service or perseverance.

Our motto, "Giving Honor Where Honor Is Due," is more than a tagline. It is a promise to report with care, to verify before we publish, and to celebrate generosity wherever we find it, from the firehouse to the front porch.

This premiere issue covers the work of the Tim Tebow Foundation against human trafficking, the opening of Intrada Westside in Atlanta, and the national conversation around Fostering the Future. Each story was checked against official sources before it reached these pages, and each one carries its source so you can check it too.

We are an online magazine now, with a paper edition planned for January 2027. Wherever you are reading, the standard does not change: real people, honest reporting, and honor given where honor is due.

Paula Skipper PeeplesChief Editor, Heroes & Sheros Magazine

    The organization behind the mission

    Faith, hope and love, in three disciplined parts

    Reporting from the foundation's public record

    Founded in 2010, the Tim Tebow Foundation describes its mission as bringing faith, hope and love to people facing some of the darkest times in their lives, with a focus on what it calls the Most Vulnerable People around the world.

    One of the foundation's major areas of work is the fight against human trafficking and child exploitation. The approach has three parts: prevention through education and training, rescue through partnerships that support rescue missions, and care for survivors as they rebuild their lives.

    Survivor care includes communities of safe housing designed to help people return to everyday life with dignity. The foundation works with partners in the United States and abroad, and publishes its programs and priorities on its official site.

    Heroes & Sheros features the organization because the work matches our mission: honoring the people and organizations that serve vulnerable communities quietly, consistently and without asking for attention.

    The full story continues with the numbers behind the programs, the partners involved, and what the foundation says comes next. Sources for every claim appear on the closing page of this feature.

    The block behind the ribbon

    A Heroes & Sheros sample feature | Facts checked, interviews pending

    An Atlanta music legend and a Grammy-winning singer-songwriter spent a November morning in 2023 cutting a ribbon on a development that became 143 new homes. The project, Intrada Westside, opened as a 100-percent affordable community, with 25 units set aside for permanent supportive housing.

    T.I., born Clifford Joseph Harris Jr., is an Atlanta rapper, actor and entrepreneur. Tameka "Tiny" Harris is a singer and songwriter who rose to prominence as a member of the group Xscape. Between them are decades of music, business and a family life lived, mostly, in the city they call home.

    The development gave the neighborhood something harder to photograph than a podium: density with dignity. Every unit was built as affordable housing, which in Atlanta terms means rents tied to incomes rather than to the market, and the supportive quarter was designed for residents leaving situations that steady housing usually solves first.

    At the ribbon cutting, T.I. stood with Mayor Andre Dickens and the project's partners. Public records from Atlanta Housing describe the development and its timeline; local coverage from that week recorded the speeches, the crowd and the length of the line for cake.

    "Giving honor where honor is due: some honors are built, not awarded." Heroes & Sheros editorial Sample line

    What makes the story worth a magazine's attention is not the guest list. It is the accountability: when a public figure puts a name to a ribbon, the numbers afterward belong to the record. Units opened, units occupied, rents held, repairs made. This magazine intends to return to those numbers in future issues.

    Heroes & Sheros presents the project as a community-impact story: an entertainment figure investing presence, not just publicity, in a neighborhood that shares his history. It also stands as the kind of story the magazine exists to tell, where honor attaches to the work itself.

    This sample feature withholds personal anecdotes from earlier drafts because they could not be independently documented. Names, dates and housing details above are checked against Atlanta Housing and public reporting, and the interviews that fill out the human chapters are scheduled for the print edition. Sample copy

    Fostering the Future: what the record showsContinued from page 03 | Reported from official sources

    For Heroes & Sheros readers, the throughline of this story is simple: what happens the moment foster care ends? A young person aging out of the system faces a first apartment, a first paycheck and a first month with nobody behind them. The national conversation around Fostering the Future is, at heart, a conversation about that month.

    November 2025: the order

    The White House announced an Executive Order titled "Fostering the Future for American Children and Families," describing an initiative led by First Lady Melania Trump focused on expanding education and opportunity for youth in foster care. The order set out priorities that included mentorship, career readiness and support for young people leaving care.

    June 2026: the accounts

    The White House announced the launch of Fostering the Future Accounts, described as a savings and investment vehicle for youth in foster care, developed with the U.S. Department of Treasury. Accounts were announced with pledges from private partners, and the program's public materials list education, training and housing as intended uses.

    Because this story concerns a current political figure and a government program, Heroes & Sheros reports it as a factual record: the order, the accounts and the pledges, each linked to its official source. No endorsement is implied or offered, and claims that could not be verified against official pages were left out of this article entirely.

    Why it matters here

    The magazine covers foster care because foster families and the young people they raise are on our beat list, alongside teachers, first responders and the mentors who show up on weekends. Georgia readers know the numbers at the state level; national reporting gives the numbers a name.

    What follows in the print edition is a reported feature with the young people themselves: what they were told, what they were given, and what they built anyway. Those interviews are in progress, and every participant will review their own quotes before publication. Sample article frame

    Our standard

    Stories about current political figures are published as factual reporting with clear sourcing and without political endorsement. Names, dates and claims are checked before publication, and corrections run in the next issue and online.
